# Everyday Machine Lab: second survey (39 new candidates)

Date: 2026-10-23. Status: research only. No code changed.

## How to read this file

- This file adds 39 candidates. None of them repeats the 43 in docs/machine_survey.md or the 14 tabs built or in build.
- Excluded on purpose (already in survey 1): espresso, pressure cooker, kettle, induction hob, vacuum cleaner, brakes/ABS, suspension, loudspeaker, elevator, quadcopter, planetary gear, transformer, touchscreen, QR code, solar PV, Stirling engine, hydraulic jack, water hammer.
- Score = T (teaching) + W (wow) + V (validation) - E (effort). Each is 1 to 5. Effort: 1 to 2 low, 3 medium, 4 to 5 high.
- Validation tags: **[src]** = a URL in the Sources list was returned by a search during this survey. Search snippets only; I did not read full pages. **[calc]** = I computed it from an exact formula. **[known]** = textbook value from author memory, not checked. **[est.]** = estimate. Treat [known] and [est.] as unverified.
- Popularity ranking: web search found no reliable per-video ranking (see Sources). The W and T scores are author judgement. Channel-level figures came from third-party counters and may be old.
- Presets: O = optimal point, E1 and E2 = edge cases.

## Top 10: short notes

1. **Tuned mass damper (12).** A swaying tower is a clear 3D hook. One extra mass with two settings (mass ratio, tuning) shows a big drop in sway. The model is two coupled ODEs and is cheap. Risk: the real building periods need an input source. Validate against the Den Hartog formulas first.
2. **Rocket (11).** Users build stages and see why orbit needs about 9.4 km/s. The exponential in the rocket equation is a strong teaching point. The ODE with drag and gravity turn runs in real time. Risk: Isp sources disagree, so show Isp as an input with a range.
3. **Roller coaster (11).** Drag a track point and watch g-force change. Energy method is exact, so the model is trivial. The clothoid loop shows why real loops are not circles. Overlap with nothing.
4. **Golf ball (11).** The drag crisis is a surprising result: a rough ball flies farther. A Cd(Re) table plus Magnus lift is enough. Compare smooth against dimpled in one flight. Digitise the Cd curve from a published source before release.
5. **Gyroscope (11).** The axis moves sideways under gravity. The formula is exact, so validation is clean. Add a spin-down slider to show the toppling moment. Pair it with the bicycle tab for stability.
6. **Fibre optic (11).** Best-validated item: attenuation sheets are public. Bend loss and critical angle give a hands-on hook. Single-mode versus multimode explains why the internet backbone uses glass.
7. **LED vs incandescent (11).** The Planck curve and the eye response curve explain why a filament wastes 90% or more as heat. CIE V(lambda) is a public table. Compare a spectrum with a lumens-per-watt bar.
8. **Differential (11).** One gear set explains why a car turns. Make one wheel stand on ice and watch the open diff spin it. Add LSD for contrast. Kinematics are exact.
9. **Hot-air balloon (11).** A simple, calculable lift (0.258 kg/m3 at 100 C vs 20 C). The envelope and burner give a clear 3D view. Altitude control teaches stability.
10. **Pelton wheel (11).** The u = v/2 optimum is a clean result and a nice mirror of the pump tab. A dam-head slider gives scale. Peak efficiency needs a published check.

Runners-up: eyeglasses (N11, overlap with camera), grid frequency (N12), Wi-Fi (N13), train wheelset (N14, highest wow among medium-effort items).
